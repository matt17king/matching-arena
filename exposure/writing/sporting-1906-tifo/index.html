Sport

Sporting showed Manchester United who made their heroes.

Before kick-off in Lisbon, Sporting's fans unveiled a display for the club's 120th year with a young Cristiano Ronaldo at its centre. Here's how a selling club turns the players it lost into its proudest story.

Manchester United went to Lisbon on Wednesday for a Champions League game. Before a ball was kicked, the home fans had already made their point. Across one end of the Estádio José Alvalade, a giant display went up: the number 1906, the club's greatest players, and in the middle, a teenage Cristiano Ronaldo in a Sporting shirt.

What happened

The display marked 120 years since Sporting was founded in 1906. Ronaldo was at its centre, drawn wearing number 28, the shirt he wore in his one season in Sporting's first team before he left for United in 2003. Around him were other club greats, including former captain Sebastián Coates and Bruno Fernandes, who was on the pitch that night as United's captain.

Nani, who also went from Sporting to United, was in the stands. The game finished 2-2, with Fernandes on the scoresheet for United.

Why this opponent, of all opponents

The timing wasn't an accident. It was at this stadium, in a friendly to open the new Alvalade in 2003, that United first saw Ronaldo up close and decided to sign him. Fernandes followed the same road years later. The two clubs hadn't met in a competitive game since 2007.

So Sporting's fans used the one night United were in town to remind them, and everyone watching, where those players came from.

If you can't keep your stars, own the fact that you made them.

A selling club with a story to tell

Most clubs outside the richest handful sell their best players. That's the model. The usual way to feel about it is loss: he was ours, and then he left.

Sporting tells it the other way round. We made Ronaldo. We made Bruno. We made Nani. That turns every sale into proof that the club is good at something very few are: making great footballers. It's a story that helps the academy recruit the next one, makes sponsors proud to be attached, and gives fans something no transfer can take away.

It's the heart of idols sell, influencers inform. The idol carries the club's name with him wherever he goes, if the club keeps the connection alive.

Two nights, the same lesson

The night before, Manchester City's fans sang for Kevin De Bruyne as he returned in a Napoli shirt. Different clubs, different countries, the same instinct: fans stay loyal to people. The clubs that do well are the ones that go along with that feeling and don't fight it.

The takeaway

Your history is an asset. The players who left are still part of it, and they're still carrying your name.

What clubs and brands can steal from it

  1. 01

    Claim what you made

    If your people go on to bigger things, say so, loudly and proudly. It's proof of what you do well.

  2. 02

    Pick your moment

    Sporting waited for the one opponent that made the message land. Timing made it a story.

  3. 03

    Put the past and the present together

    The display ran from 1906 to players still playing. A history should feel alive, not like a museum.

  4. 04

    Keep the door open

    Nani was in the stands. Former players who feel welcome become ambassadors for life.

  5. 05

    Let the fans say it

    A message from the stands carries more weight than one from the marketing team.
