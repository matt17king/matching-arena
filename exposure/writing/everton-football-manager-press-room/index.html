Everton put a Football Manager fan in their real press room.

A fan held a pretend Everton press conference in his bedroom and went viral. Everton gave him the real room, and the real captain. Here's why the best club content this month cost almost nothing.

By Matt King22 November 20264 min read

In this piece

  1. What happened
  2. Why it worked
  3. The cheapest great content in football
  4. Fans are the best creative department
  5. What any club or brand can steal from it

ShareLinkedIn ↗X ↗

A 37-year-old Everton fan sat in his bedroom, in front of a homemade club backdrop, and held a press conference for a team he only manages on Football Manager. His wife walked in and reminded him he was a grown man with two children. He told her to take it up with Tarkowski. The clip went viral. Then Everton did the best thing a club can do with a moment like that: they made it real.

What happened

The fan posts online as The Fat Centre Back. With the new Football Manager just out, he set up his room like Everton's media suite: club backdrop on the screen behind him, microphone in front, managing the Toffees in the game and answering imaginary journalists between matches.

Then his wife and daughter walked in. She reminded him he was "a grown adult" with two kids, sitting in his bedroom playing Football Manager and pretending he had a squad. He didn't break character. He pointed at an empty chair and told her: "Tell that to Tarkowski." Then he politely asked them to leave his press room.

Everton saw it. They invited him to hold his press conference for real, in the media room at Hill Dickinson Stadium. He sat down at the desk, and the door opened. James Tarkowski, Everton's captain, walked in with "Morning, gaffer." The fan, lost for words, called him "skipper". Tarkowski told him a teammate had shown him the video, and that people around the club had been talking about it. Everton posted the meeting on 18 November, and it spread around the world, from Liverpool to Luxembourg to Argentina.

Why it worked

It was already a great story. Everton didn't have to invent anything. A fan had written the setup, the punchline and the character himself. The club just wrote the ending.

It was a joke everyone was in on. Millions of people play Football Manager, and plenty of them have done a version of this: talked to the screen, rehearsed a team talk, sulked after a defeat. The wife's line landed because everyone has heard it, or said it.

And the pay-off was perfect. The empty chair in the bedroom was "Tarkowski". Turning that empty chair into the actual Tarkowski is the kind of callback you can't script in a meeting. It only works because it answers the fan's own joke.

The fan wrote the setup and the punchline. Everton just wrote the ending.

The cheapest great content in football

This cost Everton a room they already have, an hour of their captain's time and a camera crew they already employ. No agency, no campaign, no media spend. And it did more for how people feel about the club than most paid campaigns will this season.

That's the lesson of the clipping economy. Most of the football people see isn't made by clubs. It's made by fans and creators, posting from bedrooms, sofas and stands. The clubs that do best don't try to compete with that. They watch it, and when something brilliant comes up, they join in.

It's also how a club makes a fan for life. That supporter will tell this story for the rest of his days. So will his daughter. And every fan who watched it now knows that if you love Everton loudly enough, Everton might notice.

Fans are the best creative department

There's a bigger idea here. Fans are already making content about your club every single day. Some of it is funnier, rawer and more shareable than anything an in-house team could make, because it comes from real love, not a brief.

Most clubs treat that as noise. The smart ones treat it as a free creative department. Everton's media team didn't come up with the idea. They spotted it, and that's a skill too: someone at the club was watching what fans were posting, and was trusted to move fast enough to catch it while it was still hot.

The takeaway

The best club content often starts with a fan. Your job isn't always to have the idea. It's to spot the right one and give it a better ending.

What any club or brand can steal from it

  1. 01

    Watch what your fans make

    Have someone whose job is to follow fan accounts, creators and comments every day, not just your own posts.

  2. 02

    Move while it's hot

    This worked because Everton replied within days. A month later, the moment has gone. Give the team permission to act fast.

  3. 03

    Answer the joke, don't replace it

    The best reply is a callback. Everton didn't make a new video; they finished the fan's one.

  4. 04

    Use what you already have

    A press room, a captain and a camera. The most loved moments rarely need a budget.

  5. 05

    Make the fan the hero

    Tarkowski played the supporting role. The fan was the star, and that's why other fans shared it.
