Man City put a fashion label on the pitch.

City walked out in the Champions League in an orange kit made with C.P. Company. Here's why the smartest fashion launch of the week happened during a football match.

On Tuesday night, in front of a Champions League audience, Manchester City walked out against Napoli in bright orange. It wasn't a normal change kit. It was made by PUMA with C.P. Company, the Italian fashion label, and it was the first time anyone had seen it.

What happened

City teased "a special kit" the day before. Then, on 24 November, the 2026/27 fourth kit made its debut on the pitch at the Etihad. It's a solid orange, a colour City last wore in 2016/17, with an uneven fade across the shirt and shorts that makes it look worn-in, as if it came out of C.P. Company's archive. The crest on the chest is a joint one, made for this collaboration. The sponsor logos are in black.

It will be worn for selected Champions League games. A wider collection goes on sale around the world on Tuesday 1 December, with a presale for fans the day before at the City Store.

Why C.P. Company makes sense

This isn't a random pairing. C.P. Company has been City's official fashion partner since 2024/25, dressing the players, coaches and directors for European trips. Until now that meant jackets and travel wear. This is the first time the label has made it onto the match shirt.

And the label has history in the city. In the mid-1980s, C.P. Company spread through football crowds across the north-west of England. Its Goggle Jacket, with lenses built into the hood, became part of terrace culture. So when City put that name on a shirt, it isn't borrowing fashion credibility from outside football. It's bringing something home.

The best collaborations don't borrow from another world. They bring something home.

The launch was the smart part

Most fashion launches happen in a showroom or on a feed. City launched this one in the middle of a Champions League night, on the bodies of its players, in a game the whole of Europe was already watching. And not just any game: it was the night Kevin De Bruyne came back to the Etihad, so the cameras were never going to look away.

Then came the wait. You couldn't buy it that night. You have to wait a week, with a presale first for fans who turn up in person. That gap turns a kit into something people talk about, ask about and plan to get.

The side door, in orange

This is the side door working in both directions. Someone who loves the label but has never watched City now has a reason to look. Someone who loves City gets something they'd wear on a night out, not just to a match. It's the same thinking as F1 signing Nike: make the sport something people wear, and it reaches people a broadcast never will.

The takeaway

A match is the best catwalk a club has. Millions are already watching, and the models are the players.

What clubs and brands can steal from it

  1. 01

    Pick a partner with roots in your world

    C.P. Company already belonged to northern football culture. The fit was there before the deal was.

  2. 02

    Build up to the shirt

    Two seasons of travel wear came first. By the time the kit arrived, the partnership already felt real.

  3. 03

    Debut it where everyone is looking

    A big European night beats any launch event. Use the moments you already own.

  4. 04

    Make people wait

    Show it, then sell it a week later, with a presale for fans first.

  5. 05

    Design it to be worn off the pitch

    If it only works in the stadium, it's kit. If it works on a night out, it's fashion.
