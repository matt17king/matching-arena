The Miami Heat become Vice City.

The night before GTA VI launched, the Heat turned their arena into Vice City: the court, the shirts, even the burgers. Here's why borrowing the year's biggest culture moment beats fighting it.

GTA VI is set in Vice City, Rockstar's version of Miami. So on the night before the game came out, the Miami Heat stopped being the Miami Heat. They played the Milwaukee Bucks as Vice City: on a court built for one night, in neon Vice City shirts, with Burger Shot serving the food.

What happened

The Heat teamed up with Rockstar Games for "A Night in Vice City" at Kaseya Center on 18 November, the eve of GTA VI's global launch. The team built a court for one game only, in the look of the game. The players wore black Vice City shirts designed with Nike and Travis Scott's Cactus Jack, with "Vice City" in pink neon script and a GTA VI patch. Fans could buy them, in limited numbers, on the night.

The whole arena joined in. Food stands were rebranded as Burger Shot and Cluckin' Bell, the fast-food chains from the game. Cars from GTA VI sat outside for photos, the game's music played all night, and the whole thing went out live on ESPN.

Rockstar even sent the Heat players custom consoles, controllers and copies of the game as a thank-you. As one Heat executive put it, the aim was "a crossover experience of gaming and sport in a way that no one has actually interacted with before."

Why it worked

It borrowed the biggest moment of the year instead of competing with it. In launch week, GTA VI is what a huge share of young people are talking about. Most teams would see that as competition for attention. The Heat saw it as a wave to ride.

It gave gamers a reason to watch basketball. Plenty of the people counting down to GTA VI have never sat through an NBA game. For one night, the game they love was on the court. That's a door into the sport that no normal broadcast could open.

And it gave Heat fans something to collect. A one-night court and a limited shirt turn a regular-season game in November into an event people will say they were at.

Don't fight the culture's biggest moment for attention. Become part of it.

Miami was always Vice City

This didn't come from nowhere. Vice City has always been Miami. The original GTA Vice City was a love letter to the city's 80s neon, and so was Miami Vice before it.

The Heat have been leaning into that look for years. Their "Vice" City Edition shirts, first worn in the 2017-18 season, became some of the best-selling alternate shirts in NBA history. The team says it sold nearly 230,000 of them, worth around $25 million. When a club already owns a look, a moment like this is the pay-off for years of building it.

The side door, at full scale

This is the side door in action. Reach people through what they already love, then put the sport in that world. Here the world was Vice City, and the sport was standing right in the middle of it.

It works the other way round too. Rockstar put its game in front of a live NBA audience the night before launch, and it had already put its ads on the boards at El Clásico in October. Sport is one of the best places to reach people who love games, and the best game launches know it.

It's also the same wave as Central Cee hiding a song on the GTA VI radio. The biggest launches now spill out of the game and into music, fashion and sport.

What football can steal from it

Football clubs get moments like this all the time: a huge album drop, a film set in their city, a game launch, a local artist going global. Most let them pass by.

  1. 01

    Know the culture moments coming to your city

    Game launches, album drops, film premieres and festivals. Put them in the calendar like fixtures.

  2. 02

    Pick the fit that's already there

    The Heat and Vice City share a city and a look. The best crossovers feel obvious the moment you see them.

  3. 03

    Go all in, for one night

    The court, the kit, the food, the music. A half-hearted tie-in looks like an advert. A full takeover becomes a memory.

  4. 04

    Make it collectable

    Limited shirts and one-off moments give fans something to keep, and something to post.

  5. 05

    Let the partner bring their fans

    A partner's audience can be far bigger than any team's. The deal should send their fans your way, not just their logo.
