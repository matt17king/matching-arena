AI is the pit crew, not the driver.

AI changes the tyres fast. It doesn't drive the car. How I use it in sports marketing, what it can't do yet, and why effort isn't the same as authenticity.

By Matt King18 November 20263 min read

  2. The driver still decides the race
  3. Effort isn't authenticity
  4. Where it goes wrong
  5. How I run it

Every marketing team is being told to use AI for everything. Most of the advice is either "it will replace you" or "it's just a tool". Neither helps much. The way I think about it comes from Formula 1: AI is the pit crew. It is not the driver.

The pit crew is essential

An F1 pit stop takes a couple of seconds. Around twenty people, each with one job, done perfectly and at speed. Nobody watches a race for the pit crew, but nobody wins without one.

That's what AI is brilliant at in marketing: the jobs that need doing fast, often and right.

  • Research. Pulling together what a market, an audience or a competitor looks like, in minutes instead of days.
  • Versions. Taking one idea and making it work in ten sizes, five languages and three tones.
  • Reporting. Turning raw numbers into something a team can read and act on.
  • Automations. Moving data between tools so nobody has to copy and paste it.

I use Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Make.com for this every week. It gives back hours that go into the work that actually matters.

The driver still decides the race

What AI can't do yet is make the creative call. It can tell you what has worked before. It can't tell you why a footballer wearing a twinset sold out a menswear range that influencers couldn't move. That came from understanding who the audience looked up to, not from data.

The real skill in this job is taking something that worked in one place and landing it somewhere new: a sneaker idea in Japan, a music moment in Brazil, a creator series in the Middle East. That needs taste, timing and a feel for culture. That's the driver.

AI can tell you what worked. It can't tell you what's next.

Effort isn't authenticity

Here's the part most people miss. When making content becomes cheap, audiences stop rewarding effort. Anyone can now produce a polished video, a slick graphic or a thousand words in seconds. So polish stops meaning anything.

What people reward instead is the stuff that can't be faked: access, taste and truth. The real tunnel walk. The real reaction on the bench. The moment nobody could have planned. Fans can feel the difference between something made because it mattered and something made because it was easy.

That's why the best use of AI is to buy back time for the human parts, not to replace them.

Where it goes wrong

  1. 01

    Everything starts to look the same

    If every team asks the same tools the same questions, they get the same answers. Taste is what makes you different.

  2. 02

    Speed replaces thinking

    Making ten versions in a minute is only useful if the first one was right.

  3. 03

    The voice disappears

    Athletes and talent are the storytellers. AI-written words in their mouths read as an advert, and fans notice.

How I run it

  1. 01

    Pit crew

    AI for research, versions, reporting and automations. Fast, precise and always checked.

  2. 02

    Driver

    People for the brief, the casting and the creative call. Always.

  3. 03

    Telemetry

    Test, learn and scale. Google Analytics, Meta Ads Manager and Klaviyo tell us which idea earned the next lap.

Get that order right and AI makes a good team much faster. Get it wrong and it just makes an average team louder.
