Man City sang for the player in the other shirt.

Kevin De Bruyne walked out at the Etihad in a Napoli shirt, and the home fans sang his song. Here's why the way a club treats its former stars is some of the best marketing it will ever do.

On Tuesday night Kevin De Bruyne came back to the Etihad for the first time as an opponent, playing for Napoli in the Champions League. He was on the other side now. The home fans sang his name anyway.

What happened

Before kick-off on 24 November, City supporters gave De Bruyne the kind of welcome usually kept for their own players. The "Oh Kevin De Bruyne" chant, which rang around the stadium for a decade, came back. A banner showed him in a crown and robes under the words "King Kev".

Cards were handed out in the stands, showing De Bruyne in a City shirt. One carried the message: "Our King. If we know you're happy every day, that's enough for us." Another read: "Once a Blue, always a Blue."

The club joined in too. City posted a video of him arriving at the stadium, all smiles and hugs with old colleagues, with a two-word caption: "Welcome back", a sky-blue heart and a crown.

Then they played the game. It finished 2-2.

Why he earned it

De Bruyne arrived from Wolfsburg in 2015 with doubts hanging over him after a spell at Chelsea that hadn't worked. He left ten years later as the creative heart of the best side in the club's history.

Six Premier League titles, two FA Cups, five League Cups and the Champions League. When he left in May 2025, City gave him a giant display in the stands, a mural in the city and a road at the training ground with his name on it.

Fans follow people, not just badges

This is the part clubs sometimes forget. A supporter's love for a player doesn't end when the contract does. The shirt changes; the feeling stays. For ten years De Bruyne was the reason a lot of people watched City at all, and those people didn't stop caring when he moved to Naples.

It's the heart of idols sell, influencers inform. The idol is what people attach to. A club can either fight that when the idol leaves, or be generous about it.

The shirt changes. The feeling stays.

Why being generous is the smart move

City and its fans could have treated him as the enemy for ninety minutes. Plenty of clubs would. Instead the night became a story about a club that looks after its own, and that story travelled further than a 2-2 draw ever would. Italian papers led on the welcome. Fans of other clubs shared the banner.

It also says something to the players in the home dressing room, and to the ones City wants to sign: give this club your best years and you will be loved for them, wherever you end up.

And notice who started it. The chant, the banner and the cards came from the stands, and the club followed their lead. As with Everton and the Football Manager fan, the best moments often start with supporters. The club's job is to make room for them.

The takeaway

How you treat people after they leave tells everyone how you'll treat them while they're there.

What clubs and brands can steal from it

  1. 01

    Treat a return like a homecoming

    When a former star comes back, plan for it. A welcome costs nothing and is remembered for years.

  2. 02

    Let the fans lead

    The banner and the cards came from supporters. Give them the space, and the materials if they need them.

  3. 03

    Keep your legends in the story

    A mural, a road name, a seat at the big games. The relationship shouldn't end with the contract.

  4. 04

    Think about who is watching

    Current players and future signings see how the last hero was treated. So do sponsors.

  5. 05

    The same goes for brands

    Ambassadors and partners move on. Speak well of them when they do, and their fans will keep speaking well of you.
