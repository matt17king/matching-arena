Taylor Swift, the Chiefs and the side door.

There was no campaign. Taylor Swift went to watch Travis Kelce, and a whole new audience followed her into the NFL. Here's why it worked, and what to steal from it.

The best sports marketing moment of the last few years wasn't planned by a sports marketer. In 2023, Taylor Swift started turning up to watch Travis Kelce play for the Kansas City Chiefs, and millions of her fans suddenly had a reason to watch American football.

The play

In September 2023, Taylor Swift sat in a box at Arrowhead Stadium to watch the Chiefs. The broadcast cut to her again and again. Within days she was the biggest story in the NFL, and she hadn't touched a ball.

She kept coming back. Every appearance became a moment: what she wore, who she sat with, how she reacted. Travis Kelce's shirt sales jumped. The NFL's own social accounts leaned into it rather than pretending it wasn't happening.

By the time the Chiefs reached the Super Bowl that season, the game had become one of the most watched broadcasts in American history.

Why it worked

Her fans had a reason to care. Plenty of Swifties had never watched an NFL game. Suddenly someone they already loved was in the story, so the story was worth following.

It didn't feel like an advert. Nobody was paid to post. It was a real relationship playing out in public, and that made it far more powerful than any campaign could have been.

And the sport said yes. The NFL and its broadcasters could have ignored it, or rolled their eyes. Instead they made room for it, and made new viewers feel welcome.

People don't get into a sport through the sport.

The side door, in one example

This is the clearest example of the side door I know. The front door of the NFL is the fixture list, the rules and the fantasy league. To someone who has never watched, that door is shut.

The side door was Taylor Swift. Her fans walked in through something they already loved, then found a sport inside. Some of them stayed for the football.

You don't need Taylor Swift

Most sports will never get a moment this big. But the pattern works at every size. Every new audience already follows someone: a musician, a creator, a fashion figure, a local hero. The question is whether your sport is anywhere near them.

At The Couture Club, the menswear only sold once footballers wore it. Different direction, same lesson: the person carrying the message decides who listens. More on that in idols sell, influencers inform.

Steal this

  1. 01

    Ask who your new audience already follows

    Not who is famous. Who they actually care about.

  2. 02

    Look for real connections

    The best moments come from relationships that already exist: a player who loves an artist, a creator who grew up supporting a club.

  3. 03

    Say yes when culture turns up

    When a moment happens, lean in. Make new people feel welcome, not tested.

  4. 04

    Give them a way to stay

    The moment gets them in. A story, a player to follow or a reason to watch next week keeps them.
