EA brought John Motson back, and asked his family first.

EA Sports FC 27 has added the late John Motson's commentary, rebuilt with AI and his family's blessing. Here's why it's a useful test of how to use AI on something people love.

  2. They asked first
  3. Not everyone is comfortable
  4. Why I think they got it mostly right
  5. What clubs and brands can steal from it

For a generation of football fans, John Motson's voice was the sound of the game, on the BBC and on the PlayStation. He died in 2023. This week, players of EA Sports FC 27 heard him again, describing players he never saw.

What happened

On 26 November, EA added Motson to FC 27 as an optional commentator, sitting alongside the game's current broadcast teams. He was the voice of EA's football games from 1996 to 2006, so the studio had almost ten years of his recordings to work from.

What it didn't have was Motson saying the names of today's players. So the new commentary is a mix: his original recordings, plus new lines made by an AI tool trained on his voice. EA calls it "a digital tribute to one of the game's greatest voices".

They asked first

This is the part that matters. EA worked with Motson's family from the start, and reports say the family was paid for the rights to his voice. Mat Prior, a senior game design director at EA, told the BBC: "There was no way we would have moved forward with this in any way, shape or form if they weren't fully on board."

His son, Fred Motson, said: "My father adored being the voice of the game, and we are so grateful to the team at EA Sports FC for this touching tribute and preserving his memory in-game."

The technology was the easy part. The permission was the work.

Not everyone is comfortable

It would be wrong to pretend everyone loves it. Some players have called it uncomfortable, and worse. Peter Caulfield, who runs the campaign Save Our Voices Now, argues that nobody's voice should be cloned unless they agreed to it themselves while they were alive, and that a family's consent isn't the same thing.

That's a fair challenge, and it won't be the last time football hears it. EA has made two choices that help: you have to choose to switch Motson on, and Prior was clear that "there are certainly no plans to replace anyone with AI."

Why I think they got it mostly right

I've written before that AI is the pit crew, not the driver. This is that idea in practice. The thing fans love here is Motson: the voice, the excitement, the sheepskin coat they picture when they hear him. The AI didn't create any of that. It filled the gaps so that something real could reach people who never heard it the first time.

Compare that with the lazy version: an invented voice, built to save money, with nobody asked. Same technology, opposite result. What makes the difference is where it starts. EA started with a person people love and the family who speak for him, and only then reached for the tool.

The takeaway

Use AI to carry something people already love further. Don't use it to replace the thing they love.

What clubs and brands can steal from it

  1. 01

    Start with the thing people love

    A voice, a player, a kit, a chant. If the idea doesn't begin there, AI won't fix it.

  2. 02

    Ask first, and pay fairly

    Get the blessing of the people it belongs to before you build anything, and make it a real partnership.

  3. 03

    Say plainly how it was made

    EA said which parts are original and which are AI. Being open is what stops a tribute feeling like a trick.

  4. 04

    Make it a choice

    Fans can switch Motson on or leave him off. Nobody has it forced on them.

  5. 05

    Expect the argument, and listen to it

    Some people will be uneasy. Take the criticism seriously, and be ready to say what you won't do.
