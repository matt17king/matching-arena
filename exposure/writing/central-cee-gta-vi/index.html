Central Cee, GTA VI and the song you have to find.

GTA VI is here, and Central Cee has a new song on its radio that isn't out anywhere else. No countdown, no big announcement. Just a track waiting for fans to find it. Here's why that's such a smart play.

GTA VI has finally arrived, thirteen years after GTA V. Millions of people are driving around Vice City this week with the radio on. And somewhere on Vice City Rap Radio, between the other tracks, is a new Central Cee song called "Rock N Roll" that you can't get anywhere else.

What happened

As the game landed, players started hearing a Central Cee track nobody recognised. "Rock N Roll" plays on Vice City Rap Radio, the game's main rap station. It isn't on Spotify or Apple Music. Within hours, UK rap accounts were posting clips of it with one message: it's only on GTA VI.

There was no big build-up that I can find. No countdown, no billboard, no teaser. The song was simply in the game, waiting for someone to tune in and notice.

He isn't the only artist using the game as a stage. Playboi Carti is reported to have an original song on the same station, and there's talk of a UK takeover segment featuring Alhan FM. Rockstar has kept the full tracklist quiet until launch, which turns every new song into a discovery.

Why it works

It turns fans into the marketing team. A song you're told about is an advert. A song you find is a story. The first person to hear it gets to be the one who posts it, and everyone else wants to hear it for themselves. That's how a track travels further than any announcement could take it.

It plays where the audience already is. Players will spend hundreds of hours in Vice City, and every drive is another chance to hear it. In GTA, the radio isn't a side feature. For a lot of players, it's how they discover music in the first place.

And scarcity does the rest. If the only place to hear a song is inside the biggest game in the world, the game becomes the place to be. Fans have to go looking, and looking is what makes them care.

A song you're told about is an advert. A song you find is a story.

Rockstar has done this before

This isn't the first time GTA has premiered music. In 2021, GTA Online's The Contract update put six unreleased Dr. Dre tracks inside the game, with Eminem, Snoop Dogg, Anderson .Paak and more. They stayed exclusive to the game for around two months before they reached streaming.

Games have become one of music's biggest stages. In 2020, Travis Scott premiered a new song with Kid Cudi during his concert inside Fortnite, in front of millions of players. The audience is huge, it's young, and it's paying attention.

The side door, for music

I usually talk about the side door as a way into sport: reach people through what they already love, then put the sport in that world. This is the same idea, pointed at music. Millions of people love GTA. Central Cee has put his music inside the thing they love, instead of asking them to come and find him.

It's the clipping economy at work too. The song spreads through clips that fans record and post. The game is the source. The clips are the marketing.

What sport can steal from it

  1. 01

    Hide something worth finding

    A secret kit detail, a hidden message in a trailer, a song in a game. When fans find it themselves, they share it like it's theirs.

  2. 02

    Launch where the audience already is

    Not on your own channels first. Inside the game, the creator's stream, the show they already watch.

  3. 03

    Let the fans break the news

    Don't announce everything. Leave room for discovery, then celebrate the people who found it.

  4. 04

    Make it exclusive, for a while

    A short window where something only exists in one place gives people a reason to go there. Then release it widely and let it run.

Football has all the raw material for this. Kits, chants, walk-out songs and stadium moments are exactly the things fans love to find first. The clubs that hide the good stuff, and trust their fans to find it, will get far more out of it than the ones that announce everything in a press release.
