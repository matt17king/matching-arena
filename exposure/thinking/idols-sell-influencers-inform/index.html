Idols sell, influencers inform.

Influencers tell people a product exists. Idols make people want to be part of it. If you need people to buy, cast an idol.

In this piece

  1. What happened
  2. Why it works
  3. The idol has to fit
  4. How I cast

By Matt KingNovember 20262 min read

At The Couture Club, influencers sold the womenswear. The menswear didn't move until footballers wore it. Then it sold out.

What happened

At The Couture Club, the womenswear grew with influencers. They posted, their followers bought, and it worked.

So we ran the same play for the menswear. Influencers posted, people saw it, and not much happened. Then footballers started wearing it. Players in the store, players arriving at training in it. The menswear sold out. Working with Molly-Mae Hague and Phil Foden, reach went up 600%.

Same brand, same quality, same price. The only thing that changed was who was wearing it.

An influencer is someone you watch. An idol is someone you want to be.

Why it works

Influencers are brilliant at information. They tell you something exists, show you how it fits and where to get it. That gets people to notice.

Idols work on identity. Wearing what they wear says something about who you are. A lot of young men don't take style cues from fashion creators. They take them from the players they grew up watching. That gets people to buy.

The idol has to fit

This isn't about the biggest name. Lamine Yamal and Bad Bunny are both huge. Neither works unless the idea fits who they are. The right voice for the idea beats the biggest name that doesn't fit it.

How I cast

  1. 01

    Decide the job first

    Awareness, or sales and belonging? Influencers inform, and they are efficient at it. For sales, find an idol whose world matches the product. Often you need both, in that order.

  2. 02

    Give them their voice

    Talent are the storytellers. My job is the brief and the platform, not their script. If you have to script them, you picked the wrong person.

  3. 03

    Every pick needs a reason

    A surprising pick can be the best one. A pick nobody can explain never is.

  4. 04

    Borrow the audience you want

    Choose partners for the people who follow them, especially the ones who don't watch the sport yet.

  5. 05

    Local heroes travel

    Lead with who each market already loves. Ohtani in Japan opens doors no global advert can.

The full list is in the eleven rules I cast talent by.
