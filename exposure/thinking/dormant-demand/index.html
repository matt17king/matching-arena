Dormant demand.

Dormant demand is the gap between how many people care about a sport's people and how many watch the sport itself.

In this piece

  1. The gap nobody measures
  2. You can see it everywhere
  3. Why it stays asleep
  4. How to wake it up
  5. Measuring it

By Matt KingNovember 20266 min read

Millions of people already care about sport's people. Most of them never watch the sport. That gap is the biggest opportunity in sports marketing.

The gap nobody measures

Sport is very good at counting the people who turn up. Tickets, viewers, subscribers, followers of the official account. It is much worse at counting the people who care but never turn up.

They follow a player. They watch the press conference clip. They wear the shirt because it looks good. They know the rivalry from the group chat. They have never sat through a full match. That isn't a lack of demand. It's demand with nowhere to go.

You can see it everywhere

Cristiano Ronaldo has more than 600 million followers on Instagram. Only a small slice of them watch the league he plays in. They care about him, not the fixture list.

When Caitlin Clark moved to the WNBA, people who had never watched women's basketball started tuning in to watch her. The league didn't create those fans from nothing. They were already there, following a person.

When a player becomes a national story, like Alexandra Eala in the Philippines, a whole country starts caring about tennis it never used to watch. The interest came first. The player gave it somewhere to go.

And a press conference line, a tunnel fit or a reaction shot can travel further than the match it came from. The attention is real. It just isn't pointed at the product yet.

Why it stays asleep

  1. 01

    Sport sells the fixture. People buy the person.

    Most sport marketing leads with dates, times and kick-offs. New fans are drawn to personalities, rivalries and stories.

  2. 02

    The door is the wrong size.

    Ninety minutes, a subscription and a rulebook is a lot to ask of someone who is only curious.

  3. 03

    It lives in the wrong place.

    A sport's own channels mostly reach people who already follow the sport. The dormant fans are somewhere else.

How to wake it up

  1. 01

    Find it

    Look outside your own numbers. Who follows your players but not you? What gets searched, clipped and shared? Who wears the shirt and has never been to a game?

  2. 02

    Name who they are

    Work out what they love instead: an artist, a creator, a fashion scene, a national hero. That is where they spend their time.

  3. 03

    Build a smaller door

    Don't start with the full match. Start with a clip, a fit, a creator series, a shorter format. Something they can say yes to today.

  4. 04

    Give them a reason to come back

    A story with a next episode. A weekly ritual. A side to be on. That is how curiosity turns into a habit.

Finding the door is the next idea: culture is the side door. Who you send through it is the third: idols sell, influencers inform.

Measuring it

I'm building a simple, public measure of dormant demand: how many people follow and search for a sport's people, compared with how many watch the sport. It will be built only from public numbers, and I'll publish it here once a year. The first edition is in the works.
