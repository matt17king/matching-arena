The clipping economy.

In the clipping economy, fans and creators, not the people who own the rights, share most of the sport that people actually see.

In this piece

  1. Where people really watch
  2. Streamers worked this out first
  3. What sport can learn
  4. The balance

By Matt KingNovember 20264 min read

Most people never see a full match. They see a clip, cut by someone else, posted somewhere else.

Where people really watch

For a lot of people, sport is a ten-second clip on a feed. A goal, a reaction, a line from a press conference, a moment in the tunnel. They didn't choose a broadcaster. Somebody else cut it, captioned it and posted it.

Streamers worked this out first

The biggest streamers don't rely on people watching for hours live. They rely on clippers: fans and paid editors who cut the best moments into short videos and post them everywhere. Some streamers pay clippers by the view. The stream is the source. The clips are the marketing.

The stream is the source. The clips are the marketing.

What sport can learn

  1. 01

    The clip is the shop window

    Most new fans meet a sport through a clip before anything else. That is where dormant demand first wakes up.

  2. 02

    Decide which moments should travel

    The personality, the reactions, the off-pitch moments. Those are marketing. Make them easy to share.

  3. 03

    Always leave a route back

    Every clip should make the full thing easy to find. Otherwise it is attention with nowhere to go.

The balance

Live sport is valuable because it's protected, and that matters. This isn't about giving the match away. It's about choosing which moments are the advert, and letting those go as far as they can.
