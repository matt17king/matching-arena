Plays worth stealing.

Four moves from other people's playbooks: what they did, why it worked, and what to take from it.

F1 × Netflix

01 / 04The film room

Drive to Survive

The play

F1 let the cameras into the paddock and handed the story to Netflix: the rivalries, the team bosses, the drivers as people.

Why it worked

It reached people who watch long-form drama, not motorsport. They met the characters first, and then they wanted the races.

Steal this

Find where your future fans already spend their hours and tell the story there. Content made for new audiences beats paid ads aimed at old ones.

Music × NFL

02 / 04The film room

Taylor Swift × the Chiefs

The play

No campaign at all. Taylor Swift started turning up to watch Travis Kelce, and the broadcasts followed her.

Why it worked

Her fans suddenly had a reason to care about a game many had never watched. Someone they already loved was in the story.

Steal this

Culture is the side door. Ask who your new audience already follows, then put the sport in that world.

Tennis × Sneakers

03 / 04The film room

Alcaraz × Travis Scott × Nike

The play

Carlos Alcaraz, tennis's It Boy, previewing Travis Scott's Nike tennis shoe.

Why it worked

A sneaker drop travels further than a match report. It put a tennis player in front of the sneaker world, on that world's terms.

Steal this

Treat athletes like the culture figures they are. The tunnel, the warm-up and the drop are all a stage.

Football × Creators

04 / 04The film room

Baller League

The play

Six-a-side football with its own rulebook, built for streams and short clips, with creators and famous faces in the dugouts.

Why it worked

It didn't ask young fans to sit through 90 minutes. It met them in the formats they already watch.

Steal this

The format is the product. Change the format and you change who turns up.
